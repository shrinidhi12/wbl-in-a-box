# Objection playbook

### "We don't have time."
**Reply:** "Completely understand. Most of our partners are small teams too."
**Smaller ask:** A 20-minute conversation with one student, or curriculum advice for a teacher (Level 1).
**Proof point:** "Tom at Front Range Auto started with one career fair afternoon."

### "What about liability?"
**Reply:** "Great question. For on-campus activities, students are under school supervision the whole time."
**Smaller ask:** Start with something at school, like a guest talk or career fair.
**Proof point:** For later levels, the school walks you through the paperwork. *Confirm specifics with your district.*

### "Teens aren't reliable."
**Reply:** "That's fair. We've all seen that. Our students come prepared, and a teacher is always there."
**Smaller ask:** Mock interviews, where you'll see students at their best.
**Proof point:** Share a student thank-you note from another partner.

### "We tried this before and it didn't work."
**Reply:** "I'm sorry. What happened?" (Listen.) "Here's what we do differently: one contact, and we follow through."
**Smaller ask:** A one-time event with a clear agenda.

### "We're too small."
**Reply:** "Small businesses are some of our best partners. Students love seeing how a business really runs."
**Smaller ask:** A guest talk about starting a business.

### "Talk to HR."
**Reply:** "Happy to! Could you introduce me, or forward this one-pager?"
**Smaller ask:** Offer to join a short call with HR together.

## Practice role-play
**Employer:** "Honestly, we're slammed through December."
**You:** "Totally get it. What's your slow season?"
**Employer:** "January's pretty quiet."
**You:** "Perfect. Could I put you down for a 30-minute virtual Q&A with our business class in late January?"
**Employer:** "Sure, that I can do."
